# Observed drill protocol

**Purpose.** Show, with real people and real time, what happens when a heat protection fails: how long until it
is really back, and whether someone other than the person who fixed it confirms it in time. The same failure is
handled with the team's usual way of working and with Bardan, with the same people, resources and safety
procedures.

**What this is not.** It is not a site trial, not a study of heat illness and not a health outcome. A few drills
show the workflow working (or not) under drill conditions. Results are reported drill by drill, with no
percentage improvement and no statistics that small numbers cannot carry.

**Why it replaced the simulation.** An earlier version showed a simulated comparison with an alert-only workflow.
Its result followed from its own assumptions (Bardan was assumed to be faster and better at catching false
completions), so it was removed. What the software does in each situation is now shown as scripted checks of the
real code (Trust Centre, *Drills and comparison*), and timeliness only from drills like this one.

---

## 1. Design

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Comparison | The same failure, handled with the **usual way of working** (radio, phone, walking over, whatever the team normally does) and with **Bardan** |
| Order | Counterbalanced A B B A (generated by the console, with a random first arm), so learning during the session does not favour one arm |
| Drills | At least 2 per way of working (4 in total, about 60 to 75 minutes with briefings). More is better; 3 per arm if time allows |
| Failure | One failure for the whole series: the **water point at Zone A runs dry** (recommended) or the **shaded rest area is full** |
| Target | Agreed before the first drill and locked by the console when it starts: 20 minutes for water, 25 for shade, to have it **back and independently confirmed**. A drill target, not a legal limit |
| False report | In one drill of each arm, chosen at random by the console and kept from the participants, the person fixing it is asked privately to report it fixed without fixing it |
| Same conditions | Same people in the same roles, same place, same resources, same safety rules, same target in both arms |

## 2. People

| Role | Who | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| Observer (facilitator) | Runs the drills and records times. Takes no part in the workflow | Guest reviewer persona, drill console |
| Worker | Notices the failure and raises it | Ravi (English) or Anwar (Arabic) |
| Supervisor | Fixes it | Joseph |
| HSE lead (optional) | Independent check | Sara |

Minimum three people (observer, worker, supervisor). With three, the worker's own confirmation is the
independent check. Participants must be adults who agreed to take part after hearing what the drill involves.
No names, photos of faces, phone numbers or health details are recorded anywhere. Anyone can stop at any time.

## 3. Safety

* Role-play only. **Nobody goes without water and nobody works in heat for a drill.**
* Run it indoors or in shade, in comfortable conditions. The "empty water point" is a clearly marked empty
  container labelled **EMPTY**; real drinking water stays within everyone's reach throughout. For the shade
  scenario, a sign saying **FULL** on a set of chairs is enough.
* No running, climbing, carrying heavy loads or use of tools. Walking between two points is enough.
* Stop at once if anyone feels unwell or uncomfortable, and record the drill as stopped (it is then left out of
  the results).
* If an exercise is ever run on a real site, the site's HSE lead approves it in advance and it never interrupts real
  protection (real water and shade stay available, and real heat rules apply as usual).

## 4. Before the first drill

1. Open the app, choose **Guest reviewer**, open **Drills** and set up the series: failure, drills per arm,
   target, number of people, languages, and whether to include the false reports (recommended).
2. The console creates a separate drill sandbox at 07:00 on the scenario day with the plan active. Invite each
   participant with their link or QR code. Each person opens it on their own phone.
3. Put the marked container (or the FULL sign) in place. Agree who stands where: the worker near Zone A, the
   supervisor some distance away (another room or the end of a corridor), the HSE lead elsewhere.
4. Read the safety rules aloud and confirm everyone agrees to take part.

## 5. Running one drill

1. **Brief.** Read aloud the briefing the console shows for this drill (which way of working, and what the
   failure is). In a false-report drill, the console also shows a **private instruction**: tell the person who
   will fix it, privately, to report it fixed without fixing it, and to fix it only if someone challenges it.
2. **Start.** Say "start" and press **Start drill** at the same moment. From now on the sandbox clock runs in real
   time. In a usual-way drill, participants' screens are covered ("put this phone away").
3. **Observe and record.** Tap each moment when you see or hear it:
   * *Reported to someone responsible*
   * *Fix started*
   * *Reported as fixed*
   * *Really back: I saw it* (always the observer's own observation, never taken from the app)
   * *Confirmed by someone else* (someone other than the person who fixed it checks that it is back and says
     so in the way the workflow allows: in Bardan the worker's confirmation or an HSE check; in the usual way,
     any check the team normally does)
   * *False report caught* (false-report drills only: the workflow flagged that it was not really done)

   In a Bardan drill the ledger fills these moments itself (marked *from the ledger*); the observer's own tap
   always takes precedence. A wrong tap can be undone.
4. **End** when the control is back and confirmed, or when the team stops acting. Add a short note (no names).
   A drill left running ends automatically after 60 minutes.
5. **Reset** the container (or the sign) for the next drill. The console closes the drill's task so the next drill
   starts from a clean site.

## 6. What is measured

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Minutes until really back | From "start" to *Really back: I saw it* |
| Minutes until back and independently confirmed | From "start" to the later of *Really back* and *Confirmed by someone else*. A confirmation more than one minute before the control was really back is a **false confirmation**, not a verification |
| Within target | Back and independently confirmed within the agreed target |
| False reports caught | In false-report drills: whether, and how fast, the workflow flagged that it was not done |
| App used during usual-way drills | Any report or task action in the app during a usual-way drill (it should be zero) |

Results are shown drill by drill. Medians with ranges are shown only across drills without a false report; with
two drills per arm that is one drill each, and the table says so.

## 7. Publishing the results

1. On the console, press **Export results (JSON)**. A copy is also kept on the observer's device after each
   drill. The export carries a SHA-256 hash of its content.
2. To publish with the release, run:

   ```bash
   cd backend
   python scripts/publish_drill.py path/to/bardan-drills-drs_xxxx.json --where "Role-play, 4 adults, English and Arabic"
   ```

   The script refuses any file whose hash does not match, or whose results do not follow from the recorded
   moments. It stores the export unchanged in `shared/drills/published.json`, with the date and a short
   description of the conditions.
3. The Trust Centre shows published drills under *Drills and comparison*. It re-checks every export when it loads
   and shows a problem instead of the numbers if anything does not check out.

Report in the submission exactly what was observed: the number of drills, the conditions, each drill's times and
whether it was within the target. Never round in Bardan's favour, never drop a drill except one stopped for a
recorded reason, and say what went wrong.

## 8. Limitations

* A handful of role-play drills with friends or colleagues are not a site: real sites are larger, noisier and
  busier, and people behave differently when observed.
* Learning effects: participants get faster as the session goes on. The A B B A order spreads this across both
  arms but does not remove it.
* The prototype has no push notifications: the supervisor's screen refreshes every few seconds during a drill and
  shows a banner (and vibrates where the phone allows) when a new report arrives. A deployed system would also
  send a push notification or SMS.
* The observer's taps are a few seconds late at most; ledger times are exact. Both are kept with their source.
* The comparison is only as good as the "usual way" the team brings. If the team has no usual verification step,
  the usual-way arm will show "not independently confirmed": that is a finding about the workflow, not a flaw of
  the drill.
